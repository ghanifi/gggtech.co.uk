Why a managed programme

Hotels are a target. Security has to be continuous.

Hotels hold exactly what attackers want: card payments, passport and contact details, and a constant flow of staff, guests and contractors using shared devices and networks. Front desks are busy, staff turnover is high and systems run around the clock, which makes phishing, credential theft and ransomware easier to pull off. Our article on the five critical cybersecurity threats facing UK hotels covers the most common attack routes.

Security tools only work if someone configures, watches and updates them. This programme bundles our cyber security services, firewall management and PCI DSS compliance support into one managed service with a fixed monthly fee, so security is owned and maintained every day rather than bought once and forgotten.

It is delivered by the same UK-based team that has supported hospitality IT since 2010 and looks after more than 500 hotels today.

Compliance update

PCI DSS v4.0 is now fully in force

The requirements that PCI DSS v4.0 originally marked as future-dated became mandatory on 31 March 2025. For hotels, the most significant include multi-factor authentication for all access into the cardholder data environment, technical controls to detect and protect against phishing, and targeted risk analyses that justify how often certain controls are carried out.

If your last PCI review was completed before that date, your evidence may no longer reflect what your acquirer expects. The programme keeps your controls and documentation current year-round. For a deeper look at the standard, see our hotel PCI DSS compliance service.

Problems it prevents

Hotel security problems the programme prevents

Most hotel breaches start with something ordinary: a convincing email, a shared login or a device nobody remembered to update.

Backups are your last line of defence against ransomware. Pair this programme with managed backup and disaster recovery so you can restore cleanly if the worst happens.

Front desk phishing

Fake booking requests and "guest complaints" with malicious attachments target reception. Filtering, MFA and training cut the risk.

PMS account misuse

Shared logins and leavers who still have access. We enforce individual accounts, MFA where supported and prompt removal of old users.

Exposed payment terminals

Terminals on the same network as guest WiFi bring everything into PCI scope. Segmentation keeps cardholder data isolated.

Guest WiFi attacks

Poorly isolated guest networks let devices see each other. Client isolation and separate networks stop that.

Forgotten key card and building systems

Door lock servers and CCTV recorders are often unpatched. We bring them inside the security perimeter. See access control.

Ransomware at check-in peak

An encrypted PMS on a busy arrivals day is the worst case. Endpoint protection, monitoring and tested backups limit the damage.

FAQ

Managed hotel cyber security: frequently asked questions

How is this different from your cyber security service?

Our cyber security service page describes the individual capabilities. This programme bundles them, including monitoring, firewall, endpoint protection, PCI DSS support, training and incident response, into one managed service with a fixed monthly fee and a single SLA.

Will this make our hotel PCI DSS compliant?

Compliance is ultimately the merchant's responsibility and is validated through your acquirer, usually by a self-assessment questionnaire or, for larger merchants, an assessment by a Qualified Security Assessor. We help you scope, remediate and maintain the technical controls and evidence so that validation is straightforward.

What does PCI DSS v4.0 change for hotels?

Requirements previously marked as future-dated became mandatory on 31 March 2025. They include multi-factor authentication for all access into the cardholder data environment, technical anti-phishing controls and targeted risk analyses for how often certain controls are performed.

What happens if we suffer a cyber attack?

Call our 24/7 line. We follow the agreed incident response plan: contain the threat, preserve evidence, restore operations from clean backups and support you with notification duties. Under UK GDPR, a reportable personal data breach must be notified to the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware of it.

How much does managed cyber security cost?

It is a fixed monthly fee, quoted per property after a free assessment. The fee depends on the number of devices, users, sites and your PCI DSS scope.

Do you replace our existing firewall and antivirus?

Not necessarily. If existing equipment is supported and fit for purpose, we manage it. Where it is end-of-life or unsuitable, we explain why and recommend replacements.
