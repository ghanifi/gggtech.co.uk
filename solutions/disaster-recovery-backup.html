Knowing your backups work, not hoping they do

Most hotels have some form of backup. Far fewer know whether it actually works, how long a restore would take, or what the front desk should do while systems are down. This programme answers those questions, and keeps answering them every month.

The essentials

RPO, RTO and the 3-2-1 rule, in plain English

Three ideas shape every backup and recovery plan. We agree each one with you, system by system, during onboarding.

RPO

Recovery Point Objective

The maximum amount of data you can afford to lose, measured in time. An RPO of one hour means backups must run at least hourly, so a failure never costs more than an hour of bookings or postings.

RTO

Recovery Time Objective

How quickly a system must be back in service after a failure. Your PMS usually needs a much shorter RTO than an archive file share, and the plan reflects that priority.

3-2-1

The 3-2-1 backup rule

At least three copies of your data, on two different types of storage, with one copy off-site. We add an immutable copy that ransomware cannot encrypt or delete.

Managed backup and DR vs a backup drive in the office

A USB drive or NAS beside the server feels like protection until the day it is needed. Here is the difference in practice.

  Managed backup & DR "We have a backup drive"
Backup status Monitored daily, failures fixed Assumed to be working
Copies 3-2-1 with an immutable copy A single copy, usually on the same site

FAQ

Hotel backup and disaster recovery: frequently asked questions

What is the difference between backup and disaster recovery?

Backup is having copies of your data. Disaster recovery is the plan, tools and people that get your systems running again from those copies within an agreed time. This programme covers both, plus the front desk procedures that keep the hotel operating in between.

What are RTO and RPO, and which do we need?

RPO (Recovery Point Objective) is how much data you can afford to lose, measured in time. RTO (Recovery Time Objective) is how quickly a system must be back in service. You need both, and they differ by system. We agree them with you during onboarding.

Our PMS is in the cloud. Do we still need backup?

Your PMS vendor is responsible for their platform, but check what your contract says about data retention and restoration. You will still have local and cloud data the vendor does not protect, such as finance, email, file shares and key card or POS servers, and you need connectivity to reach the PMS. We review vendor terms and cover the gaps.

How often do you test restores?

Test restores are scheduled at least quarterly and after any significant change, such as a PMS upgrade or server replacement. You receive a short report each time.

Will backups protect us against ransomware?

Backups are your last line of defence. Following the 3-2-1 rule with an immutable or offline copy means attackers cannot encrypt or delete every copy. For prevention, combine this programme with managed cyber security.

How much does managed backup and disaster recovery cost?

It is a fixed monthly fee, quoted per property after a free assessment. It depends on how much data you hold, how many systems are covered and the recovery targets you need.
